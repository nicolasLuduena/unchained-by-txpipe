### Three pasteable pitch sentences

1. "Tokenized real-world assets are a measured thirty-nine billion dollars on-chain today, stablecoins excluded; tokenized Treasuries alone went from under one billion to fourteen billion in under three years, and the most conservative projection on the table, McKinsey's, is two trillion by 2030."

2. "Argentina just did what Brazil did in 2022 and Japan did in 2020: it made tokenized securities legal for retail and left the resale market to be built. Japan waited three and a half years for a secondary venue, Brazil's only licensed one trades on Wednesdays, and Argentina's sandbox has thirteen months left."

3. "The piece nobody built in Brazil, Japan or Dubai is a shared resale layer across wallets. Every secondary market abroad is one venue owned by one operator. Título XXII allows up to five synchronized PSAV venues per issue, and Cuota is the synchronization."

### Do not say

1. Do not quote US$16 trillion, US$18.9 trillion or US$30 trillion as if measured. They are projections from 2022, 2025 and 2024 respectively; the measured figure is US$39.4 billion. BCG/Ripple's figure also counts stablecoins and tokenized deposits, which the others exclude.
2. Do not call the EU DLT Pilot Regime a success. After 26 months it had three venues and EUR 11.9 million of live share issuance; ESMA itself asked for the caps to be lifted.
3. Do not say "tokenized Treasuries grew because of regulation". No US rule changed in 2024. Growth came from 5 percent policy rates, BlackRock's entry and one issuer (Hashnote USYC, zero to US$1.6 billion in 2024).
4. Do not say the UK sandbox has live trading. As of 2 November 2026 three firms are approved to go live and the first issuance is expected in 2027.
5. Do not say "nobody built resale" in Argentina. Volsmart runs a venue for its two issues; the claim is "no shared, cross-PSAV layer with open, verifiable contracts" (settled in #1).
6. Do not say Drex is live or that Brazil has a CBDC. The Drex pilot's second phase ended in November 2025 and participants expect nothing before 2027.
7. Do not quote Securitize's US$5.3 billion quarterly "transaction volume" as secondary trading. It is a vendor figure that mixes subscriptions, redemptions and transfers.
8. Do not say 24/7 trading is the norm abroad. Japan's START matches orders twice a day; BEE4 trades one day a week; SDX moved its bond trading into SIX Swiss Exchange hours in June 2025. Only Dubai (Prypco) and tZERO (23.5 hours on business days) are near round-the-clock.
9. Do not quote rwa.xyz's "represented asset value" (US$392 billion) as on-chain value. It counts assets that are referenced but not freely transferable on-chain.
10. Do not say Hong Kong proves retail demand. The HK$10 billion bond was bought by institutions from a sovereign issuer.
11. Do not say Landtoken I raised US$20 million. That is the authorized ceiling; the placed amount is not public in anything found.
12. Do not say "compliant" (from #1). Say "built to the structure of Título XXII and operated by a PSAV".

## Part 3. Jurisdiction cards

Headings are identical on every card. "Before and after" rows show the number before the rule or event, the number after, both dates, and the confounders.

### 3.1 Brazil

- **Rule and date.** CVM Resolução 88 of 27 April 2022 (crowdfunding, in force 1 July 2022 per the consolidated text's own effective date; superseded Instrução 588). CVM Ofício Circular SSE 4/2023 (4 April 2023) and 6/2023 (5 July 2023): receivables and fixed-income tokens are securities and may be offered through Res. 88 platforms. CVM Resolução 29 of 11 May 2021 created the regulatory sandbox. CVM Resolução 249/2026 created a simulated DLT pilot with no real investors (secondary). Drex (central bank tokenized real) is not law and not live.
- **Who may issue.** Under Res. 88: "sociedade empresária de pequeno porte" with gross revenue up to R$40 million in the prior year; maximum target R$15 million per year; 180-day window; additional lot up to 25 percent. Securitization companies use the same regime for receivables tokens. The CVM is consulting on raising the cap to R$25 million (R$50 million for registered securitizadoras); not yet published as of the sources found (Cointelegraph Brasil, 2026-08-12).
- **Who may run secondary.** Res. 88 art. 15 lets a crowdfunding platform intermediate buy and sell transactions in securities it previously placed, but art. 15 §1 forbids "sistema centralizado e multilateral de negociação", any order-registration environment and any market maker, and §2 forbids the words "bolsa", "mercado de balcão" and "mercado secundário". A real secondary market needs an organized-market licence (CVM Res. 135) plus a depository licence (CVM Res. 31). BEE4 obtained both after graduating from the sandbox (BEE4 site footer, primary; Cointelegraph via TradingView, secondary, states 2025).
- **Transfer restrictions.** Res. 88 securities are not admitted to regulated markets; secondary distribution offers capped at 20 percent of the target; controllers may sell at most 5 percent of their stake through the platform. BEE4 shares trade only through BEE4 via participating brokers (Itaú, Genial; XP as listing consultant).
- **Investor caps.** R$20,000 per calendar year per non-qualified investor across all Res. 88 offers (art. 4), waived above R$200,000 of financial investments or income (consolidated text). BEE4's tickets start at R$2,000 (InfoMoney, secondary).
- **Sandbox end date.** Res. 29 authorizations are temporary; first cycle admitted four proposals (30 September 2021 and 27 December 2021); BEE4's term was extended to June 2024 (BlockNews via BEE4 site, secondary) and it then left the sandbox with definitive licences. CVM: "no momento, não temos previsão para a abertura de um novo processo de admissão" (CVM sandbox page, primary).
- **Flagship issues.** Engravida (BABY1), first BEE4 listing, first session 28 September 2022; Mais Mu; Plamev Pet; Eletron Energia (R$3.3 million, March 2024, originated by XP). Total raised across the four: R$17,094,501 (BEE4 letter to CVM, 2026-01-23).
- **Before and after numbers.** (a) Crowdfunding under Res. 88: 403 offers and R$1.2 billion in 2024 versus 861 offers and R$3.9 billion in 2025, after Ofícios 4/2023 and 6/2023 opened the regime to tokenized receivables. Confounders: securitization offers were about 70 percent of operations and 76 percent of amount already in 2024 (Capital Aberto, secondary), so the growth is receivables tokens, not equity; Brazilian policy rates were in double digits, favouring fixed-income tokens. (b) BEE4 secondary: R$331,775 and 18,475 tokens on the first session (28 September 2022); about R$3.5 million over 70 sessions by March 2024 (NeoFeed, secondary); R$3 million cumulative by January 2026 (BEE4 letter; lower than NeoFeed's earlier figure, so one of the two counts differently); over R$7 million over 175 sessions by March 2026 (BEE4 ESG report). Confounder: a single venue with four listings and a weekly auction.
- **Venues.** BEE4 (organized OTC market and depository, São Paulo). Res. 88 platforms (69 authorized per CVM via Finsiders, secondary) may only run bilateral bulletin boards.
- **Trading hours.** BEE4: one session per week, Wednesdays 12:00 to 20:00 with a pre-opening auction 10:00 to 11:00 (InfoMoney 2022; NeoFeed 2024); a later BEE4 blog post gives 12:00 to 19:00 (secondary). About 8 of 168 hours.
- **Lesson for Cuota.** Brazil is the closest analogue: retail-first tokenized issuance under a crowdfunding cap, then one sandbox-born venue that owns its own order book, its own depository and its own broker connections. Volsmart's venue in Argentina is the same shape (one operator, its own app, two brokers) but without a licensed-market layer; Cuota's difference is that Título XXII already names up to five venues and a synchronization duty, which BEE4 never had to solve because it is the only venue. What Brazil forbids and Argentina allows: Brazil forbids crowdfunding platforms from running a multilateral order book (art. 15 §1); Argentina's art. 39 makes the PSAV app an authorized trading venue. What Argentina forbids and Brazil allows: Argentina bars transfers outside the designated PSAVs and to any decentralized protocol (art. 5 f); Brazil's Res. 88 securities have no on-chain transfer rule at all because the regime is not token-specific.

### 3.2 Germany

- **Rule and date.** Gesetz über elektronische Wertpapiere (eWpG), in force 10 June 2021 (text on gesetze-im-internet.de; date as reported in secondary sources, BGBl. I 2021 S. 1423). Creates electronic securities in a central register or a crypto securities register. Scope at launch: bearer bonds and (via a separate ordinance) fund units; the Zukunftsfinanzierungsgesetz extended it to shares (EY, secondary). Crypto securities register keeping is a licensed financial service under KWG § 32 (1) with § 1 (1a) 2 no. 8 (BaFin page, primary).
- **Who may issue.** Any issuer of bonds, fund units or, since the ZuFinG, registered shares, by entering the security in a crypto securities register run by a BaFin-licensed registrar. BaFin lists 8 licensed registrars as of 2026-09-28: Cashlink, DekaBank, E-SEC, Gesellschaft für Kryptoregisterführung, Hauck Aufhäuser Digital Custody, Smart Registry, SWIAT, Tradevest Digital Assets (Bitbond quoting BaFin, secondary).
- **Who may run secondary.** No eWpG-specific venue licence. Multilateral secondary trading of crypto securities needs an ordinary MTF or exchange, or an EU DLT Pilot permission; the two German DLT Pilot entities are 21X (DLT TSS, Frankfurt, 3 December 2024) and 360X (DLT MTF, 29 April 2025) (ESMA report, primary). Otherwise transfers are bilateral register entries.
- **Transfer restrictions.** Transfer by entry in the register at the holder's instruction; no statutory retail ban; issuers may restrict transfers contractually. Not found: any statutory venue allowlist comparable to art. 5 f.
- **Investor caps.** None in the eWpG. Prospectus rules apply as for paper securities.
- **Sandbox end date.** Not a sandbox; permanent statute.
- **Flagship issues.** Siemens EUR 60 million one-year digital bond on Polygon, February 2023, registrar Hauck Aufhäuser Lampe, sold directly to DekaBank, DZ Bank and Union Investment without a syndicate; settlement took two days because cash was off-chain (CoinDesk 2023-02-14, secondary; Siemens 2024 release recalls it). Siemens EUR 300 million one-year digital bond, 4 September 2024, on SWIAT's permissioned chain, settled in minutes in central bank money through the Bundesbank Trigger Solution; registrar DekaBank; investors BayernLB, DekaBank, DZ Bank, Helaba, LBBW (Siemens press release, primary via search). NRW.BANK EUR 100 million two-year bond on Polygon, 10 July 2025, registrar Cashlink, 2.16 percent coupon, anchor investor KfW (NRW.BANK press release, primary via search).
- **Before and after numbers.** Crypto securities on BaFin's list: 2 in January 2022 (both DekaBank; Bird & Bird, secondary); 43 by 13 July 2023 (BTC-Echo, secondary); 2025 and 2026 counts not found. Flagship sizes: EUR 60 million (2023) to EUR 300 million (2024) to EUR 100 million (2025). Confounders: all three are bank-placed wholesale bonds; the 2024 deal's speed came from the Bundesbank's cash leg, not from the eWpG.
- **Venues.** 21X (live, public Polygon chain, retail access without a MiFID intermediary under an exemption, per ESMA); 360X (authorised, not live at the ESMA cut-off, settles through Clearstream D7). Public trading of Siemens or NRW.BANK bonds on either: not found.
- **Trading hours.** Not found.
- **Lesson for Cuota.** Germany made the register legal by statute and got a handful of large wholesale bonds; retail and secondary trading had to wait for the EU pilot. The eWpG is the "registry" layer (what BYMA Digital Assets and the PSAVs' register do in Argentina), not a trading layer. What Germany allows that Argentina forbids: free bilateral transfer to any register-recorded holder; Argentina confines holders to designated PSAV users. What Argentina allows that Germany forbids: nothing specific found; Germany simply has no PSAV-app venue category.

### 3.3 Switzerland

- **Rule and date.** Federal Act on the Adaptation of Federal Law to Developments in DLT ("DLT Act"), adopted 25 September 2020; ledger-based securities (Registerwertrechte) in force 1 February 2021; the DLT trading facility licence in the Financial Market Infrastructure Act in force 1 August 2021 (dates from secondary sources; FINMA's DLT trading facility page, primary, describes the licence without dates).
- **Who may issue.** Any issuer may create ledger-based securities by agreement under the Code of Obligations; no FINMA authorization for the issuance itself.
- **Who may run secondary.** A FINMA-licensed DLT trading facility (which may admit retail, hold central custody, and clear and settle) or a conventional exchange plus CSD. FINMA licensed SIX Digital Exchange AG as a CSD and SDX Trading AG as a stock exchange on 10 September 2021 under the traditional licences, not the DLT trading facility licence; SDX Trading's services are "exclusively addressed to supervised financial institutions" (FINMA release, primary via search; Loyens & Loeff, secondary). Since 1 June 2025 digital bonds issued on SDX "solely trade on SIX Swiss Exchange" and SDX keeps issuance, custody and settlement (SDX news, primary via search).
- **Transfer restrictions.** Ledger-based securities transfer by ledger entry; issuers set the ledger rules. SDX is institution-only.
- **Investor caps.** None by statute. SDX: institutional participants only; the UBS bond was also held conventionally through SIX SIS so retail could buy it on the traditional exchange.
- **Sandbox end date.** Not a sandbox; permanent statute.
- **Flagship issues.** SIX Group CHF 150 million (CHF 100 million digital part A, CHF 50 million conventional part B), November 2021 (Finadium, secondary). UBS CHF 375 million three-year 2.33 percent senior digital bond, 3 November 2022, single ISIN CH1228837865, dual-listed on SDX Trading and SIX Swiss Exchange, the first native digital bond from a bank with public trading (SIX media release, primary via search). World Bank CHF 200 million seven-year digital bond, May 2024, settled in wholesale CBDC under the SNB's Helvetia pilot (Ledger Insights, secondary). City of Lugano, several bonds since 2023. Africa Finance Corporation CHF 350 million (secondary).
- **Before and after numbers.** SDX issuance: CHF 150 million (November 2021) to over CHF 1 billion (May 2024) to over CHF 2 billion (September 2026). Confounders: issuance is concentrated in a few large supranational and bank issuers; the 2024 jump coincided with the SNB wholesale CBDC pilot, which settled all five bonds of that six-month window; secondary turnover on SDX was never published and the trading function was folded into SIX Swiss Exchange in 2025.
- **Venues.** SDX (CSD and, until 2025, exchange); SIX Swiss Exchange (trading since June 2025); BX Swiss and others for other tokens (not researched).
- **Trading hours.** SDX Trading Guide v1.4 (December 2023): bonds 09:00 to 16:30 CET with a closing auction 16:25 to 16:30 (primary via search, superseded by the 2025 move to SIX Swiss Exchange hours).
- **Lesson for Cuota.** A licensed DLT secondary market for institutions ended up merging its trading into the ordinary exchange and its ordinary hours; the value that stayed on the ledger was issuance, custody and atomic settlement. Cuota should present the venue as the wallet and the settlement as the on-chain part, which is where Switzerland found the value. What Switzerland allows that Argentina forbids: issuing ledger-based securities with no prior regulator authorization of the digital representation (Argentina art. 5 g requires it). What Argentina allows that Switzerland does not offer: retail trading of tokenized securities inside a non-exchange app (art. 39); SDX is institution-only.

### 3.4 European Union

- **Rule and date.** Regulation (EU) 2022/858 (DLT Pilot Regime), applicable from 23 March 2023 (ESMA page, primary). Creates DLT MTFs, DLT settlement systems and DLT trading-and-settlement systems with exemptions from MiFID II and CSDR. Specific permissions last up to six years (recital 48, EUR-Lex, primary).
- **Who may issue.** Issuers of shares with market capitalisation under EUR 500 million, bonds and money-market instruments with issue size under EUR 1 billion (corporate bonds of issuers under EUR 200 million excluded from the count), and UCITS with AUM under EUR 500 million (art. 3(1)). Aggregate per infrastructure: EUR 6 billion at admission, transition plan triggered at EUR 9 billion (art. 3(2) and (3)).
- **Who may run secondary.** Authorised investment firms, market operators and CSDs with a specific permission from their national authority after ESMA's non-binding opinion. Authorised as of 31 May 2025: CSD Prague (DLT SS, Czech Republic, 11 October 2024, live), 21X AG (DLT TSS, Germany, 3 December 2024, live), 360X AG (DLT MTF, Germany, 29 April 2025, not live) (ESMA report, primary). A January 2026 ESMA list reportedly has six entries, four of them DLT TSS (Springer article and practitioner wiki, secondary; the list PDF was not fetched).
- **Transfer restrictions.** Instruments must be recorded on the DLT MI; 21X restricts interaction to KYC-approved wallets; CSD Prague uses owner-account segregation.
- **Investor caps.** None per investor. Retail may be admitted directly as members under art. 4(2) and art. 5(5) exemptions with informed consent; 21X uses this; 360X does not allow direct retail "at this stage" (ESMA report).
- **Sandbox end date.** Permissions up to six years each; the regime has no fixed end, but ESMA recommended in June 2025 that it be made permanent, the six-year cap removed and thresholds raised (ESMA report, section 5).
- **Flagship issues.** CSD Prague: six DLT share issues worth EUR 11,917,753 and one DLT bond of EUR 401. 21X: one DLT debt issue of up to US$500 million (ESMA report, primary). Names not given by ESMA.
- **Before and after numbers.** Authorised infrastructures: 0 at application (23 March 2023); 3 at 31 May 2025; reportedly 6 by January 2026 (secondary). Live issuance in the first 26 months: EUR 11.9 million of shares plus one bond. Confounders: CSDR art. 3(2) forced 360X to pause and redesign; thresholds "exclude many large-cap issuers" (ESMA para. 111); national authorities took a year or more per application.
- **Venues.** CSD Prague (R3 Corda, permissioned), 21X (Polygon, public chain, retail onboarding), 360X (Clearstream D7, no DLT in trading).
- **Trading hours.** Not found in the ESMA report. 21X's on-chain matching is not restricted by the regulation; its published hours were not found.
- **Lesson for Cuota.** A sandbox that caps the asset and does not give a retail distribution channel produces venues with no flow. Argentina's regime has no per-investor cap in Título XXII itself (the UVA caps are the crowdfunding modality only) and puts the venue inside wallets that already have users; that is the opposite design. What the EU forbids that Argentina allows: issuers above EUR 500 million (shares) or EUR 1 billion (bonds); Argentina has no size cap (art. 12 only requires a backup PSAV above 7,000,000 UVA). What Argentina forbids that the EU allows: transfers outside the designated venues; 21X settles on a public chain with wallet-level KYC rather than a venue allowlist.

### 3.5 United Kingdom

- **Rule and date.** Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 powers; Digital Securities Sandbox Regulations 2023; the Bank of England and FCA opened the DSS to applications on 30 September 2024 (Willkie alert, secondary, dated 2024-10-09). The DSS "is due to run until 8 January 2029" and may be extended by HM Treasury (Bank of England DSS page, primary, updated 23 December 2024).
- **Who may issue.** Any issuer of equities, corporate and government bonds, money-market instruments, fund units or emissions allowances through a sandbox entrant; derivatives and unbacked crypto excluded (Bank of England page).
- **Who may run secondary.** Sandbox entrants "established in the UK" that pass Gate 2 as a Digital Securities Depository (DSD) and/or authorised trading venue operator; Gate 3 raises limits; Gate 4 scaling; a permanent regime "to be decided" (Bank of England page; Willkie).
- **Transfer restrictions.** Securities live on the entrant's ledger; market participants interact with the entrant as with any regulated firm subject to limits.
- **Investor caps.** None per investor found; the regime is wholesale. Per-entrant live limits by asset class: ClearToken CSD's Gate 2 caps are GBP 600 million gilts, GBP 900 million GBP corporate bonds, GBP 1.8 billion non-GBP corporate bonds, equities to be confirmed (ClearToken press release 2026-09-22, primary). Fund tokenisation has firm-level AUM caps and no aggregate cap (Willkie).
- **Sandbox end date.** 8 January 2029.
- **Flagship issues.** None live yet. The first DIGIT (digital gilt) issuance is expected "early next year", i.e. early 2027, on HSBC Orion with an LSEG depository link (HSBC release 2026-07-15, primary; The TRADE, secondary).
- **Before and after numbers.** Gate 1 entrants: 2 in December 2024 (ClearToken, Montis), 17 by 9 October 2026. Gate 2 (live): 0 until 13 July 2026, then HSBC Bank Plc (13 July 2026), ClearToken CSD (18 September 2026), LSEG B3 (23 October 2026). Live issuances: 0 as of the dashboard update of 2 November 2026 (Bank of England dashboard, primary). Confounder: the first instrument is a government pilot bond announced by the Chancellor, so the first number will be a sovereign placement rather than market demand.
- **Venues.** HSBC Orion (DSD), ClearToken CSD (DSD, "24/7 settlement" claimed), LSEG B3 (DSD); trading venue operators among the Gate 1 list include BPX Markets, Tradeweb, Liquidity Digital Assets, Giltsmiths.
- **Trading hours.** Not found; no venue is live. ClearToken advertises continuous 24/7 settlement (vendor).
- **Lesson for Cuota.** The same shape as Argentina's regime (time-boxed, trading inside the sandbox, limits that rise with gates) took 25 months to approve its first live firm and has produced no issuance. Argentina already has three issues and a venue inside the sandbox; the thing to copy from the UK is the gate idea (limits that grow with evidence), the thing to avoid is wholesale-only scope. What the UK forbids that Argentina allows: nothing explicit; the DSS simply has no retail-app venue category. What Argentina forbids that the UK allows: a non-PSAV firm operating the settlement layer as a business (Argentina art. 14 pushes that to registered PSAVs).

### 3.6 United States

- **Rule and date.** No tokenization statute. Securities Act exemptions govern issuance: Rule 506(c) of Regulation D (accredited investors only, general solicitation allowed, restricted resale under Rule 144 with six-month or one-year holding periods) and Regulation A Tier 2 (up to US$75 million in twelve months since 15 March 2021, audited financials, ongoing reports; non-accredited purchase limits unless exchange-listed) (SEC guidance and Cornell LII, primary and secondary via search). Events in order: DTC tokenization pilot no-action letter, 11 December 2025, three years from launch, Russell 1000 equities, major index ETFs and Treasuries, tokens carry no collateral value inside DTC (SEC letter via search, primary; Dechert, secondary). SEC approval of Nasdaq's tokenized-securities rule change, 18 March 2026: tokenized and conventional shares trade on the same book at the same price, settle T+1 through NSCC/DTC, tokenization is a post-settlement step (CoinDesk, Ledger Insights, secondary). SEC "Innovation Exemption", 17 September 2026 (Release 2026-90, primary): temporary five-year exemption from the "exchange" definition for Tokenized Securities Venues trading tokenized NMS stock through permissioned AMM pools on public permissionless chains, with symbol and volume limits, issuer notice, halts tied to the primary exchange and public disclosure; companion "dealer" exemption for liquidity providers; no primary issuance on a TSV; no Investment Company Act relief (Sullivan & Cromwell and others, secondary).
- **Who may issue.** Anyone under registration or an exemption. Tokenized Treasury funds are private funds (BUIDL: Securitize-administered, US$5 million minimum, "qualified investors", March 2024, Fintech Futures press release reprint, secondary) or registered money-market funds (Franklin FOBXX/BENJI, registered, launched April 2021 on Stellar; US$270 million AUM at 31 March 2023, Franklin press release, primary via search).
- **Who may run secondary.** Registered broker-dealers operating an ATS (tZERO Securities, Securitize Markets, INX), national exchanges (Nasdaq from 2026), and from September 2026 exempt Tokenized Securities Venues for NMS stock only.
- **Transfer restrictions.** Reg D tokens: accredited-only and Rule 144 lockups enforced by the transfer agent's allowlist. Tokenized treasuries: whitelisted wallets only (Securitize). Nasdaq tokenized shares: same CUSIP handling through DTC pilot, conversion back to book-entry before sale (Ledger Insights).
- **Investor caps.** Accredited-only for 506(c); Reg A Tier 2 non-accredited investors limited to 10 percent of income or net worth (SEC guidance); none for exchange-listed tokenized NMS stock.
- **Sandbox end date.** Innovation Exemption expires five years after publication (17 September 2031 per law-firm summaries, secondary); DTC pilot three years from launch.
- **Flagship issues.** BlackRock BUIDL (March 2024; US$1.761 billion on 2026-11-07, rwa.xyz). Franklin BENJI plus iBENJI (US$2.945 billion combined on 2026-11-07, rwa.xyz; Stellar press release says the BENJI suite was US$1.98 billion at 29 April 2026, secondary). Ondo USDY US$2.394 billion and OUSG US$317 million. Hashnote/Circle USYC US$2.098 billion. JPMorgan JLTXX US$793 million. Securitize tokenized its own NYSE stock (SECZ) on 8 October 2026, US$186 million on-chain (rwa.xyz stocks page).
- **Before and after numbers.** (a) Tokenized Treasuries: about US$0.8 billion in January 2024, US$4.0 billion in December 2024, US$14.11 billion on 2026-11-07. No rule changed in 2024; confounders are policy rates above 5 percent (yield on idle stablecoin balances), BlackRock's entry in March 2024, and one issuer (Hashnote USYC) supplying US$1.6 billion of the US$3.2 billion 2024 increase. 2026 growth flattened (US$13.5 billion in April, US$14.7 billion in September, US$14.1 billion in November, all rwa.xyz via secondary and primary). (b) tZERO secondary: record 816,000 digital securities traded in July 2020 (tZERO release, vendor); FINRA weekly data reportedly show US$5.9 million of notional in all of 2025, almost all TZROP, and a 95 percent fall in notional since 2022 (Altstreet review, secondary, page returned 403); TZROP 24-hour volume US$33,490 with last trade 5 May 2026 (STOMarket snapshot, vendor). tZERO extended its ATS to 23.5 hours on business days with 24/7 order entry from 15 December 2025 (tZERO press release via Nasdaq, primary via search), after launching in January 2019 on Wall Street hours only (CoinDesk 2019, secondary). Hours did not create volume. (c) INX: acquired by Republic for up to US$60 million, closed 10 November 2025 (McCarthy Tétrault, secondary); its trading volume was not found.
- **Venues.** Securitize Markets (ATS and transfer agent; FINRA approval to custody tokenized securities, 2026), tZERO ATS, INX.One, Nasdaq (tokenized NMS stock, go-live date not found), TSVs under the Innovation Exemption (none named in the sources).
- **Trading hours.** Tokenized Treasuries transfer on-chain any time between whitelisted wallets; subscriptions and redemptions follow fund business days. tZERO: 00:05 to 23:35 ET Monday to Friday. Nasdaq tokenized shares: exchange hours plus T+1.
- **Lesson for Cuota.** The largest measured numbers are cash-like funds held by institutions and crypto treasuries, not retail secondary markets; the one US venue built for retail security tokens (tZERO) has close to no turnover after seven years and round-the-clock hours. Demand follows the asset, so Cuota's pitch should lean on assets Argentine retail already wants (real-estate trusts, CEDEARs) rather than on hours alone. What the US forbids that Argentina allows: public resale of private-placement tokens to non-accredited investors inside a year; Argentina's trust certificates are public-offer securities tradeable by any verified retail user on day one. What Argentina forbids that the US allows: tokens held or traded outside designated venues and on decentralized protocols (art. 5 f); the Innovation Exemption explicitly allows public permissionless chains and AMM pools.

### 3.7 Hong Kong

- **Rule and date.** No new statute; the Securities and Futures Ordinance applies. SFC circular of 2 November 2023 on intermediaries engaging in tokenised securities-related activities (primary): "existing legal and regulatory requirements governing the traditional securities markets continue to apply to Tokenised Securities" (para. 7); complexity is assessed "see-through" on the underlying (para. 24); there is "no need for a mandatory professional investor (PI)-only restriction" (para. 26), while public offers remain subject to the prospectus and offers-of-investments regimes (para. 27). A companion circular of the same date covers tokenisation of SFC-authorised funds. HKMA Project Ensemble: sandbox since August 2024; EnsembleTX pilot phase launched 13 November 2025 to run through 2026 (info.gov.hk, primary); 24/7 interbank settlement in tokenised central bank money announced 2 November 2026 with 12 banks and 20 industry pioneers (HKMA, primary).
- **Who may issue.** Any issuer under the SFO; the government itself under the Government Green Bond Programme.
- **Who may run secondary.** SFC-licensed Type 1 intermediaries; SFC-licensed virtual asset trading platforms may trade tokenised securities under "existing conduct requirements" with an SFC-approved compensation arrangement for lost tokens (circular paras. 7 and 30). Government bonds clear through the HKMA's CMU with Euroclear and Clearstream links (HKMA 2024 release).
- **Transfer restrictions.** Those of the underlying security; platform-level whitelists.
- **Investor caps.** None specific; retail allowed where the public-offer regime allows; the three government digital bonds were institutional placements.
- **Sandbox end date.** Ensemble sandbox is open-ended; EnsembleTX "will operate throughout 2026" (HKMA).
- **Flagship issues.** 16 February 2023: HK$800 million one-year tokenised green bond at 4.05 percent, Goldman Sachs platform, "the world's first government-issued tokenized green bond" (HKMA release, primary via search; Ledger Insights, secondary). 7 February 2024: around HK$6 billion equivalent in four tranches (HKD 2 billion at 3.800 percent, RMB 1.5 billion at 2.900 percent, USD 200 million at 4.749 percent, EUR 80 million at 3.647 percent), "the first digitally native bond issuance in Hong Kong", HSBC Orion, cleared through the CMU (HKMA release, primary). 10 to 11 November 2025: HK$10 billion equivalent (HKD 2.5 billion two-year at 2.5 percent, RMB 2.5 billion five-year at 1.9 percent, USD 300 million three-year at 3.633 percent, EUR 300 million four-year at 2.512 percent), orders over HK$130 billion, "the largest digital bond issuance in the world to date", first to offer settlement in tokenised central bank money (e-HKD and e-CNY), HSBC Orion (HKMA release, primary). A fourth, around HK$20 billion, was reported as planned for September 2026 (BigGo and Global Government Finance, secondary; not confirmed with HKMA).
- **Before and after numbers.** Government digital bond size: HK$800 million (February 2023), HK$6 billion (February 2024), HK$10 billion (November 2025). Orders for the third: 13 times covered. Confounders: the issuer is the sovereign, so demand is for Hong Kong credit; each issue added a settlement feature (CMU, multi-currency, CBDC) that is the HKMA's own infrastructure; no change in securities law between the three. Ensemble participants: 7 banks and 13 pioneers at launch (November 2025, Yahoo Finance, secondary) to 12 banks and 20 pioneers (November 2026, HKMA).
- **Venues.** CMU (clearing), HSBC Orion (platform), SFC-licensed VATPs (HashKey, OSL) for tokenised securities to retail where allowed.
- **Trading hours.** Secondary hours for the government bonds not found. EnsembleTX settlement: 24/7 since 2 November 2026 (HKMA).
- **Lesson for Cuota.** A regulator can be the issuer and the settlement bank, and sizes grow when the regulator keeps issuing; nothing here is a retail resale market. The reusable fact is the SFC's see-through rule: a tokenised trust certificate is no more complex than the paper one, so retail eligibility follows the underlying. What Hong Kong allows that Argentina forbids: trading tokenised securities on a licensed virtual-asset platform that is not the issuer's designated venue. What Argentina allows that Hong Kong does not provide: a statutory PSAV-app venue with no exchange licence (art. 39).

### 3.8 Singapore

- **Rule and date.** No tokenization statute; the Securities and Futures Act applies. MAS launched Project Guardian on 31 May 2022 with a first pilot by DBS, JP Morgan and Marketnode (permissioned pool of tokenised bonds and deposits on a public chain) (MAS media release, primary via search). 27 June 2024: MAS said it had worked with 24 financial institutions and completed phase one of Global Layer One (MAS release, primary). MAS Guardian page (updated 29 January 2026, primary): 28 financial institutions, 9 policymakers, 7 associations; workstreams led by Schroders (asset management), ICMA (fixed income), Ant Group and ISDA (FX); Guardian Fixed Income Framework "updated with an addendum" (date not on page). ADDX (ICHX Tech) has been a MAS Recognised Market Operator and CMS licensee since February 2020 (secondary) and is "designed for accredited investors" (ADDX site, primary).
- **Who may issue.** Any issuer under the SFA; in practice Guardian pilots by banks and asset managers; ADDX lists private funds, bonds and pre-IPO equity.
- **Who may run secondary.** RMO or approved exchange. ADDX runs a secondary platform for its own listings; Guardian is a pilot programme, not a venue.
- **Transfer restrictions.** ADDX: whitelisted accredited investors who are not US persons.
- **Investor caps.** ADDX serves accredited investors only (yearly income at least SGD 300,000, or net financial assets above SGD 1 million, or net personal assets above SGD 2 million); minimum USD 1,000 on primary and "as low as USD 100" on secondary (ADDX site and insights page, primary, vendor).
- **Sandbox end date.** Guardian has no end date and no legal sandbox status; it is a supervised pilot programme.
- **Flagship issues.** ADDX: "SGD 2B+ total transactions", "200+ opportunities" (vendor, undated, observed 2026-11-07). Guardian: UBS Asset Management tokenised VCC fund with State Street and InvestaX (secondary); Swift-settled tokenised fund subscriptions (Fintech Futures, secondary). Sizes not found.
- **Before and after numbers.** Guardian institutions: first pilot with 3 firms (May 2022), 24 (June 2024), 28 plus 9 policymakers (January 2026). Issuance or secondary volumes from Guardian: not found; MAS publishes none. ADDX cumulative transactions SGD 2 billion over about six years (vendor, undated). Confounder: Guardian counts participants, not money.
- **Venues.** ADDX; SDAX and others not researched; Marketnode (SGX and Temasek).
- **Trading hours.** ADDX secondary trading hours not found (its page describes exits "partially or fully" between investors; a third-party listing says trades can take 30 to 60 days, secondary).
- **Lesson for Cuota.** Regulator-run pilots generate participants and frameworks, not retail volume, and the one licensed private-market exchange is accredited-only. Argentina's distinctive feature is retail at US$50 to US$1,000 tickets; Singapore has no equivalent. What Singapore allows that Argentina forbids: none found at the venue level. What Argentina allows that Singapore's ADDX does not: non-accredited retail participation.

### 3.9 Japan

- **Rule and date.** Amendment to the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act passed by the Diet on 31 May 2019 (So & Sato, secondary) and effective May 2020 (World Bank paper, secondary): "electronically recorded transferable rights" (ERTR) are Type I securities, and tokenised shares and bonds are Type I securities in any case. Secondary venues need a financial instruments market licence or PTS approval. ODX received PTS approval for security tokens and opened START on 25 December 2023 (SBI release 2023-12-21, primary via search).
- **Who may issue.** Any issuer; public offerings require a Type I financial instruments business operator as distributor; self-solicitation of fund units requires Type II registration. Issuers in practice: real-estate trust banks and securities firms through Progmat (MUFG-origin) and BOOSTRY (Nomura-origin) platforms.
- **Who may run secondary.** PTS operators approved by the FSA (ODX) and Type I securities firms OTC. START trading participants are securities firms accepted by ODX (SBI Securities and Daiwa at launch; Nomura and SMBC Nikko to follow, secondary).
- **Transfer restrictions.** Resale restrictions follow the offering type (QII-only, specified investors, under-50 private placements); public-offer STs are freely transferable through participants. ODX halts trading around trust record dates (KDX notice, secondary).
- **Investor caps.** None specific to security tokens for public offerings; retail buys public real-estate STs from around JPY 100,000 (not sourced; omitted from the pitch).
- **Sandbox end date.** Not a sandbox; permanent statute.
- **Flagship issues.** Real-estate trust beneficiary STs dominate: JPY 140.8 billion (85 percent) of FY2025 issuance; seven deals above JPY 10 billion in FY2025 (BOOSTRY via NADA News, secondary). Tosei real-estate ST public offering completed May 2024 (Tosei release, primary via search; size not extracted). Corporate-bond STs JPY 20.4 billion in FY2025; ODX listed its first ST corporate bond on START in FY2025.
- **Before and after numbers.** Cumulative public ST issuance: JPY 168.2 to 168.4 billion at end-March 2025; JPY 333.3 billion at end-March 2026 (FY2025 issuance JPY 165 billion, 24 tokens; 82 cumulative). BOOSTRY forecasts JPY 200 billion in FY2026 (projection, vendor). Secondary: START first month 110 trades worth JPY 11 million (January 2024, Ledger Insights, secondary); FY2024 traded value about JPY 326 million across 6 securities (BOOSTRY FY2024, secondary); 8 tokens with JPY 33.6 billion market cap at March 2026; market cap over JPY 100 billion at 1 October 2026 (ODX, primary). Confounders: issuance doubled in FY2025 on seven very large real-estate deals, not on retail flow; secondary turnover was 0.2 percent of cumulative issuance in FY2024; the three-and-a-half-year gap between the 2020 law and the 2023 venue is the same gap Argentina has now.
- **Venues.** ODX START (PTS, Osaka). OTC through issuing securities firms.
- **Trading hours.** START: two call auctions per business day; orders 10:00:00 to 11:29:59 matched at 11:30, orders 12:00:00 to 14:59:59 matched at 15:00, Monday to Friday excluding holidays (ODX market guide, primary via search; SBI materials agree).
- **Lesson for Cuota.** Japan is the proof that issuance can grow for years before any secondary venue exists, and that when the venue arrives it can be a twice-a-day auction and still be called a market. Argentina's issuers (Landtoken, Volsmart) are at Japan's 2021 stage; the pitch line "secondary opened later than issuance" is Japan's history. What Japan forbids that Argentina allows: trading outside a licensed PTS or Type I firm (Argentina lets a PSAV app be the venue under art. 39). What Argentina forbids that Japan allows: transfer to any Type I firm's customer; Argentina confines transfers to the designated PSAVs.

### 3.10 United Arab Emirates

- **Rule and date.** Dubai: VARA Rulebooks version 2.0 published 19 May 2025, provisions effective by 19 June 2025, creating the Asset-Referenced Virtual Asset (ARVA) category under which property-backed tokens sit; issuers need a VARA Category 1 issuance licence, an approved whitepaper and, per 2026 guides, a standalone approval per ARVA (Cointelegraph, Tokenizer.estate and law-firm blogs, all secondary; the rulebook itself was not fetched). Dubai Land Department real-estate tokenization pilot launched 25 May 2025 with Prypco Mint, licensed by VARA, in the DLD Real Estate Sandbox (DLD releases, primary; Lenderkit, secondary). Phase 2 secondary marketplace opened 20 February 2026 under VARA supervision (Prypco release via Zawya, primary vendor; Gulf News, secondary). Abu Dhabi: FSRA "Guidance - Regulation of Digital Securities Activities in ADGM", announced 5 September 2019, document dated 24 February 2020 (ADGM PDF via search, primary): tokens with security characteristics are securities under FSMR and are expected to trade on an ADGM recognised investment exchange or MTF; DLT Foundations Regulations 2023 (November 2023, Registration Authority, for DAOs and foundations, not securities) (WAM, secondary).
- **Who may issue.** Dubai pilot: DLD-accredited platform (Prypco Mint) tokenizing DLD-registered title deeds; VARA-licensed ARVA issuers more generally. ADGM: any issuer of digital securities under FSRA rules.
- **Who may run secondary.** Dubai: the VARA-licensed platform itself (Prypco Mint marketplace); VARA-licensed exchanges and broker-dealers may list ARVAs (secondary). ADGM: recognised investment exchanges and MTFs.
- **Transfer restrictions.** Prypco: UAE residents aged 18 and over with a valid Emirates ID only; no cryptocurrencies accepted in the pilot; tokens trade only inside the app (Gulf News and Al Etihad, secondary; Zawya release).
- **Investor caps.** Minimum AED 2,000 per sale (Al Etihad, secondary), reduced to AED 1,000 on the secondary market in July 2026 (Lenderkit, secondary). No maximum found.
- **Sandbox end date.** The DLD phase 1 pilot ran March 2025 to February 2026 per secondary sources; phase 2 is "a controlled test" with no end date found.
- **Flagship issues.** First listing, 25 May 2025: a two-bedroom apartment in Business Bay priced AED 2.4 million, 224 investors from 44 nationalities, sold within a day (Metropolitan, secondary; DLD release of 29 May 2025 confirms "within just one day"). Second listing, 11 June 2025 release: Kensington Waters unit (AED 1.75 million per some reports), "fully funded in a record-breaking one minute and 58 seconds", 149 investors from 35 nationalities, waiting list over 10,700 (DLD release, primary). Phase 1 total: over AED 18.5 million from more than 50 nationalities (Prypco release via Zawya, primary vendor).
- **Before and after numbers.** Tokenized real-estate investment in Dubai: 0 before 25 May 2025; AED 18.5 million by February 2026 (nine months); secondary trading of about 7.8 million tokens from 20 February 2026. DLD target: tokenized assets to be about 7 percent of Dubai property transactions, about AED 60 billion, by 2033 (secondary; the DLD release says only "a central part of Dubai's property market by 2033"). Confounders: one platform, a government-sponsored launch, a residents-only waiting list of 10,700 chasing two or three listings; AED 18.5 million is about US$5 million, the same order as Argentina's two public issue amounts combined.
- **Venues.** Prypco Mint app (primary and secondary). ADGM: no live digital-securities exchange identified in the sources.
- **Trading hours.** Prypco secondary: "24 hours a day, 7 days a week" subject to compliance controls (Zawya release; Gulf News).
- **Lesson for Cuota.** Same asset class as Landtoken and Illa Belgrano, same ticket size, same retail audience; Dubai opened resale nine months after the first sale, inside the same app, 24/7, and residents-only. It is the closest in product and the furthest in law (a land registry and a virtual-asset regulator rather than a securities regulator). What the UAE forbids that Argentina allows: non-residents and anyone without an Emirates ID; Argentina's regime has no residency rule in Título XXII. What Argentina forbids that the UAE allows: nothing at the venue level; both confine trading to the licensed app.

## Part 4. Argentina comparison

### Best "Argentina just did what X did" analogues

1. **Brazil (CVM Res. 88 and BEE4).** Same continent, same retail-first crowdfunding logic with a per-investor cap in the crowdfunding modality, same sandbox-born single-operator venue with its own brokers; Argentina's Volsmart is BEE4 without the market licence, and Título XXII's five-venue synchronization rule is the thing BEE4 never needed and nobody in Brazil built.
2. **Japan (FIEA 2020 and ODX START 2023).** Real-estate trust tokens sold to retail, issuance running for years before a secondary venue, then a venue that opened three and a half years later as a twice-daily auction; Argentina is at Japan's year one with thirteen months of sandbox left, so the resale layer has to arrive faster than Japan's did.
3. **Dubai (DLD pilot and Prypco Mint).** Same asset class and ticket size as Landtoken and Illa Belgrano, resale opened inside the same app nine months after the first sale, 24/7, residents-only; it shows that retail real-estate tokens sell out when resale is promised, which is the first-sale argument in #1.

Runner-up: the UK DSS is the closest in legal shape (time-boxed sandbox with trading inside and limits that rise through gates) but has no issuance and no retail, so it is a structural comparison rather than a market one.
